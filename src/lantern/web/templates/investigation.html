What the grades and fingerprints mean

Grade is how reliable the website usually is, on the Admiralty scale used by intelligence analysts. It comes from a fixed list, not from the AI:

  • A Completely reliable — reserved for authoritative official records.
  • B Usually reliable — government and international bodies, established news organisations, universities.
  • C Fairly reliable — reference works such as Wikipedia, used as background.
  • D Not usually reliable — platforms where anyone can post, including social media ground reports. Valuable first-hand, but unverified until others confirm them.
  • F Cannot be judged — not on Lantern's list; unknown, not necessarily bad.

sha256 is a fingerprint of the exact copy Lantern saved. Changing even one character of the copy would give a completely different fingerprint, so it shows the evidence has not been altered since it was read.

Credibility (shown on each claim, 1–6) says how well the claim is confirmed: 1 confirmed by three or more independent websites, 2 probably true (two websites), 3 possibly true (one website), 4 doubtful (contradicted), 6 not assessed.
